📄 Module 1: CV Fundamentals

Learn what a CV is, how it works, what employers look for, and how to structure a professional CV.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  • Explain what a CV is.
  • Understand the purpose of a CV.
  • Identify the main sections of a CV.
  • Write a professional personal profile.
  • Understand the difference between a CV and a résumé.
  • Choose appropriate information to include.
  • Avoid common CV mistakes.
  • Prepare the foundation for your own CV.

📖 1. What Is a CV?

CV stands for Curriculum Vitae. It is a document that presents your education, skills, experience, achievements, certifications and other relevant information to an employer or organization.

A CV gives an employer a quick picture of what you can offer. Its purpose is not to tell your entire life story. Instead, it should present the information that is relevant to the opportunity you are applying for.

Your CV should make it easy for someone to understand who you are, what you have learned, what you can do and what value you could bring to an organization.

🎯 2. Why Is a CV Important?

A CV is often one of the first things an employer sees when considering an applicant.

A good CV can help you:

  • Present your qualifications professionally.
  • Show relevant skills.
  • Highlight useful experience and projects.
  • Show certifications and achievements.
  • Demonstrate that you are suitable for an opportunity.
  • Get selected for the next stage of a recruitment process.

Remember: a CV does not guarantee a job. Its main purpose is to help you present your qualifications clearly and compete for an opportunity.

🧩 3. Main Sections of a CV

A professional CV commonly contains:

  1. Contact information
  2. Professional profile
  3. Education
  4. Work or relevant experience
  5. Skills
  6. Certifications
  7. Projects
  8. Achievements
  9. Additional relevant information

Not every CV needs every section. The sections you use should depend on your experience and the opportunity you are applying for.

📞 4. Contact Information

Your contact information allows an employer to contact you.

Useful information can include:

  • Full name
  • Professional email address
  • Phone number
  • City and country
  • LinkedIn profile
  • Relevant professional portfolio or website

Use an email address that looks professional. Avoid usernames that could make your CV appear less professional.

You generally don't need to include sensitive personal information such as passwords, bank details or other private information.

✍️ 5. Writing Your Professional Profile

A professional profile is a short introduction near the top of your CV.

It should briefly communicate who you are, your strengths, your area of interest and what you are working toward.

Example

Engineering student with an interest in technology,

problem-solving and energy systems. Developing practical
skills in Python and digital tools while seeking
opportunities to gain experience and contribute to
real-world projects.

The profile should be truthful and specific. Avoid making claims about skills or experience that you do not actually have.

🎓 6. Education

Your education section shows the academic qualifications you have earned or are currently pursuing.

You can include:

  • School or university name
  • Programme or course
  • Qualification
  • Dates attended
  • Relevant achievements
  • Relevant coursework where useful

Example

Federal University

B.Sc. Engineering
2026 – Present

Relevant areas:
• Mathematics
• Physics
• Programming

💼 7. Experience

Experience doesn't only mean a formal full-time job.

Depending on the opportunity, relevant experience can include:

  • Internships
  • Volunteer work
  • School projects
  • Personal projects
  • Leadership responsibilities
  • Part-time work
  • Relevant community activities

When describing an experience, focus on what you actually did, the skills you used and the result of your work where possible.

Weak example

Helped with a project.

Stronger example

Worked with a small student team to create a basic

website and helped test the site's navigation and content.

🛠️ 8. Skills

Skills show what you are capable of doing.

Examples include:

  • Python programming
  • Microsoft Office
  • Communication
  • Research
  • Problem-solving
  • Teamwork
  • Data analysis
  • Project management

Only list skills you genuinely have. If you are still learning a skill, you can describe it honestly as a skill you are currently developing.

🏆 9. Certifications and Achievements

Certifications can demonstrate that you have completed structured training or developed knowledge in a particular area.

Achievements can include academic awards, competitions, leadership accomplishments, completed projects and other meaningful accomplishments.

Always provide accurate information. Do not claim a certification or qualification that you have not earned.

⚠️ 10. Common CV Mistakes

  • Spelling and grammar errors.
  • Using an unprofessional email address.
  • Including false information.
  • Making the CV difficult to read.
  • Including irrelevant information.
  • Using excessive decorative elements.
  • Sending the same CV to every opportunity without tailoring it.
  • Listing skills without evidence or examples.
  • Using extremely long paragraphs.

📱 11. CV vs Résumé

The words "CV" and "résumé" are sometimes used differently depending on the country and industry.

In many job applications, what matters most is whether your document clearly presents your relevant qualifications, experience and skills.

Always check the application instructions and provide the type of document requested by the organization.

📝 12. Practical Exercise

Before moving to Module 2, create a basic CV outline.

Write down:

  1. Your full name.
  2. Your professional email.
  3. Your phone number.
  4. Your current education.
  5. At least five skills you genuinely have.
  6. Any projects you have completed.
  7. Any certifications you have earned.
  8. Three achievements you are proud of.

You don't need to make the final design yet. We will improve the CV throughout the course.
