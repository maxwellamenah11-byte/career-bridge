🚀 Module 5: Getting Opportunities Through CV & LinkedIn

Learn how to use your CV and LinkedIn profile to discover opportunities, apply professionally and build your career.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  • Find relevant career opportunities.
  • Use your CV effectively when applying.
  • Use LinkedIn to discover opportunities.
  • Write professional application messages.
  • Prepare for networking conversations.
  • Recognize suspicious or misleading opportunities.
  • Track your applications.
  • Build a long-term career strategy.

🔎 1. Finding Opportunities

Career opportunities can come from many places. Don't rely on only one website or one person.

Look for opportunities through:

  • Company career pages
  • University career services
  • Professional networking platforms
  • Internship programmes
  • Career fairs
  • Professional organizations
  • Trusted job boards
  • Networking contacts

Search for opportunities that match your current education, skills and career interests.

📄 2. Using Your CV Effectively

Your CV should be ready before you begin applying.

Before submitting an application:

  1. Read the opportunity carefully.
  2. Check the requirements.
  3. Tailor your CV where appropriate.
  4. Check your contact information.
  5. Proofread the document.
  6. Use the requested file format.
  7. Submit it through the official application method.

💼 3. Using LinkedIn to Find Opportunities

LinkedIn can help you discover internships, jobs, events, organizations and people working in areas that interest you.

You can:

  • Search for relevant job titles.
  • Follow organizations you are interested in.
  • Follow professionals in your field.
  • Read industry-related posts.
  • Learn about career events.
  • Build professional connections.

Don't assume every opportunity you see online is genuine. Always verify important details.

✉️ 4. Writing Professional Messages

When contacting a professional, keep your message short, respectful and specific.

Example

Hello,

My name is [Your Name], and I am a student interested
in [your field].

I came across your profile while learning more about
the industry. I would appreciate the opportunity to
connect and learn from your professional journey.

Thank you.

Avoid sending the same long message to everyone. Personalize your message when appropriate.

🤝 5. Networking

Networking is not simply asking people for jobs. It is about developing genuine professional relationships.

Good networking can involve:

  • Learning from professionals.
  • Attending career events.
  • Joining relevant communities.
  • Sharing useful work and projects.
  • Offering help when appropriate.
  • Keeping in touch professionally.

Be patient. Professional relationships take time to develop.

⚠️ 6. Recognizing Suspicious Opportunities

Be careful when you encounter an opportunity online. Some offers may be misleading or fraudulent.

Warning signs can include:

  • Requests for unnecessary sensitive information.
  • Pressure to make immediate decisions.
  • Promises that sound unrealistic.
  • Unclear organization details.
  • Requests for unusual payments before employment.
  • Communication from suspicious accounts.
  • Links or documents you cannot verify.

When something seems suspicious, stop and verify the opportunity through an official source.

📊 7. Track Your Applications

Applying for opportunities becomes easier when you keep track of what you have submitted.

You can record:

  • Organization
  • Position
  • Date applied
  • Application link
  • CV version used
  • Application status
  • Interview date, if applicable
  • Follow-up date

A simple spreadsheet can be enough to organize your applications.

🎤 8. Preparing for Interviews

Your CV may help you reach the interview stage, but you still need to communicate your experience clearly.

Prepare to discuss:

  • Your education.
  • Your projects.
  • Your skills.
  • Your achievements.
  • Why you are interested in the opportunity.
  • Examples of problems you have solved.
  • What you hope to learn.

Be honest about what you know and what you are still learning.

📈 9. Keep Improving Your Profile

Your CV and LinkedIn profile should not remain unchanged forever.

As you complete courses, projects, internships and other experiences, update your professional information.

Think of your CV and LinkedIn profile as living records of your professional development.

🚀 10. Build Your Career Portfolio

Your CV is only one part of your professional identity.

Your career portfolio can include:

  • A strong CV
  • A complete LinkedIn profile
  • Projects
  • Certifications
  • Portfolio website
  • Professional references when appropriate
  • Evidence of your skills

The more useful evidence you build over time, the easier it becomes to demonstrate what you can do.
