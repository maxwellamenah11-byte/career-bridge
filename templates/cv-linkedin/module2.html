📄 Module 2: Building a Strong CV

Learn how to turn your education, skills, projects, certifications and experiences into a professional CV.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  • Build the main sections of a CV.
  • Present your education professionally.
  • Describe experience using strong statements.
  • Present skills with evidence.
  • Include projects effectively.
  • Present certifications and achievements.
  • Create a CV even when you have little work experience.
  • Write clearer and more professional CV content.

🎓 1. Building Your Education Section

Your education section shows employers the academic qualifications and knowledge you have developed.

For each important qualification, consider including:

  • Institution name
  • Degree, diploma or qualification
  • Field of study
  • Start and expected completion dates
  • Relevant coursework
  • Academic achievements

Example

Federal University

B.Sc. Natural Gas Engineering
2026 – Present

Relevant coursework:
• Mathematics
• Physics
• Chemistry
• Computer Programming

If you are currently studying, clearly indicate that the qualification is in progress.

💼 2. How to Describe Experience

Employers want to understand what you actually did, not just the title of a position.

A useful way to write an experience statement is:

Action + Task + Result

Weak

Worked on a website.

Stronger

Developed and tested a basic career website

using Python and Flask as part of a personal project.

Start statements with clear action words such as:

  • Developed
  • Created
  • Designed
  • Organized
  • Managed
  • Assisted
  • Analyzed
  • Researched
  • Tested
  • Presented

🛠️ 3. Presenting Your Skills

Avoid simply filling your CV with a huge list of skills. Focus on skills that are relevant to the opportunity.

Technical Skills

  • Python
  • HTML and CSS
  • Microsoft Excel
  • Data analysis
  • Computer programming

Professional Skills

  • Communication
  • Teamwork
  • Problem-solving
  • Research
  • Time management

Whenever possible, support an important skill with evidence from a project, course, experience or achievement.

💻 4. Adding Projects

Projects are especially valuable for students and people who are still building professional experience.

Projects can include:

  • School projects
  • Programming projects
  • Research projects
  • Business projects
  • Community projects
  • Personal projects

Example

Career Bridge Website

Personal Project

• Built a career development website using Flask.
• Created separate pages for courses and resources.
• Added an interactive Python practice environment.
• Deployed the website online.

A good project description tells the reader what you built, what you did and which skills you used.

📜 5. Certifications

Certifications can help demonstrate knowledge or training in a particular area.

For a certification, you can include:

  • Name of the certification
  • Issuing organization
  • Date earned
  • Credential link, when appropriate

Example

Python Programming Certificate

Career Bridge
2026

Only include certifications that you have actually earned.

🏆 6. Showing Achievements

Achievements help demonstrate results, recognition and initiative.

Examples include:

  • Academic awards
  • Competition results
  • Leadership achievements
  • Successful projects
  • Volunteer accomplishments
  • Relevant recognitions

Whenever possible, explain what you achieved instead of simply writing "hardworking student."

👨‍🎓 7. What If You Have No Work Experience?

Not having a formal job does not mean you have nothing to put on your CV.

You can highlight:

  • Education
  • Academic projects
  • Personal projects
  • Volunteer work
  • Leadership roles
  • Certifications
  • Relevant skills
  • Competitions
  • Clubs and activities relevant to the opportunity

The goal is to show what you have learned and what you can contribute, without exaggerating your experience.

🎯 8. Tailor Your CV

A strong CV should be adjusted for the opportunity you are applying for.

Read the job description carefully and identify the skills, qualifications and responsibilities the organization is looking for.

Then emphasize the parts of your background that genuinely match those requirements.

Example

If a position asks for Python and data-analysis skills, make your relevant Python projects, coursework and data experience easy to find.

📐 9. CV Structure

A simple structure could be:

  1. Name and contact information
  2. Professional profile
  3. Education
  4. Experience
  5. Projects
  6. Skills
  7. Certifications
  8. Achievements

You can change the order depending on what is strongest and most relevant to the opportunity.

📝 10. Practical Exercise

Use the information from Module 1 to create a first draft of your CV.

Your draft should contain:

  1. Contact information
  2. Professional profile
  3. Education
  4. At least three skills
  5. At least one project or relevant experience
  6. Certifications, if you have any
  7. Achievements, if applicable

Don't worry about making the design perfect yet. Focus on getting accurate and relevant information onto the page.
