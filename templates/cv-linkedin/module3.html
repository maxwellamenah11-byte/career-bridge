🎯 Module 3: CV Design, ATS & Job Applications

Learn how to make your CV clear, professional, readable and relevant to the opportunities you apply for.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  • Choose a professional CV layout.
  • Understand how Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) work.
  • Use relevant keywords naturally.
  • Tailor your CV to a job description.
  • Choose appropriate fonts and formatting.
  • Understand when to use PDF or another requested format.
  • Write a basic cover letter.
  • Review your CV before submitting an application.

🎨 1. Professional CV Design

A professional CV should be easy to read. Good design does not mean adding lots of colors, graphics or decorations.

A clear CV usually has:

  • Clear section headings
  • Consistent spacing
  • Readable fonts
  • Consistent formatting
  • Short, focused bullet points
  • Enough white space

Your design should help the reader find important information quickly.

🔤 2. Fonts and Formatting

Choose a simple, professional font that is easy to read on both computers and phones.

Keep your formatting consistent. For example, if all your job titles are bold, keep that style throughout the document.

Avoid:

  • Using many different fonts.
  • Extremely small text.
  • Huge headings that take up unnecessary space.
  • Overusing colors.
  • Long blocks of text.

🤖 3. What Is an ATS?

ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System. Some organizations use software to help organize and filter job applications.

An ATS may process information from your CV and help recruiters identify applications that match particular requirements.

You should therefore make your CV easy for both software and humans to understand.

🔑 4. Using Keywords

Keywords are important words or phrases that describe the skills, qualifications or experience relevant to a role.

For example, a job description might mention: 

Python

Data analysis
Communication
Microsoft Excel
Problem-solving

If you genuinely have these skills, make sure they are clearly represented in your CV.

Never add keywords for skills you do not actually have just to make your CV appear more relevant.

🎯 5. Tailoring Your CV

Sending exactly the same CV to every employer isn't always the best approach.

Before applying:

  1. Read the job description carefully.
  2. Identify important requirements.
  3. Compare them with your actual skills and experience.
  4. Move the most relevant information into focus.
  5. Remove or reduce information that isn't relevant.
  6. Check your CV for accuracy.

Tailoring does not mean changing your qualifications. It means presenting your genuine experience in a way that is relevant to the opportunity.

📄 6. Choosing a File Format

Always follow the employer's application instructions.

If the employer asks for a PDF, submit a PDF. If a different format is specifically requested, follow those instructions.

Before submitting a file, open it and check that the formatting hasn't changed.

✉️ 7. Introduction to Cover Letters

A cover letter is a short document that can accompany a job application when requested or appropriate.

A basic cover letter can contain:

  1. A professional greeting.
  2. The position you are applying for.
  3. Why you are interested.
  4. Your most relevant qualifications or experience.
  5. A brief closing.

Simple structure

Dear Hiring Manager,

I am writing to apply for the [Position] role.

I am interested in this opportunity because...

My experience with [relevant skill/project] has helped
me develop...

Thank you for considering my application.

Kind regards,
[Your Name]

🔍 8. The CV Review Checklist

Before submitting your CV, check:

  • Is your name and contact information correct?
  • Are there spelling or grammar mistakes?
  • Are your dates accurate?
  • Are your skills relevant?
  • Are your projects described clearly?
  • Have you tailored the CV to the opportunity?
  • Is the formatting consistent?
  • Is the file in the requested format?
  • Have you removed unnecessary information?
  • Have you checked the final document before sending it?

⚠️ 9. Common Application Mistakes

  • Submitting the wrong document.
  • Using an incorrect email address.
  • Sending a CV with spelling errors.
  • Ignoring the job description.
  • Adding skills you don't have.
  • Using an unreadable design.
  • Failing to follow application instructions.
  • Submitting without checking the final file.

📝 10. Practical Exercise

Find a real internship, volunteer opportunity or entry-level opportunity that interests you.

Then:

  1. Read the opportunity description.
  2. Write down five important requirements.
  3. Identify which requirements you genuinely meet.
  4. Modify your CV to highlight those relevant qualifications.
  5. Check the final CV for errors.

This exercise is about learning how to tailor a CV—not about claiming skills or experience you don't have.
